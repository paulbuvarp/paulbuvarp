{{- /* Person. Emitted on both language homepages. The `@id` is the important addition. Without it, two homepages each carrying an unnamed Person node are two nodes that Google has to guess are the same man. With it, both pages assert the same identifier, and anything else on the site can point at that identifier rather than describing him again. `layouts/partials/schema-talks.html` does exactly that: every talk names this @id as its performer, so eight Arendalsuka appearances attach to one entity instead of eight loose strings that happen to read "Buvarp". The fragment is anchored to the site root in both languages deliberately. There is one person, not a Norwegian one and an English one. */ -}} {{- $sameAs := slice -}} {{- range site.Params.sameAs -}} {{- with .url -}}{{- $sameAs = $sameAs | append . -}}{{- end -}} {{- end -}} {{- $schema := dict "@context" "https://schema.org" "@type" "Person" "@id" (printf "%s#person" site.BaseURL) "name" site.Params.fullName "alternateName" site.Params.alternateNames "url" site.BaseURL "jobTitle" site.Params.jobTitle "description" site.Params.description "knowsAbout" (slice "Disinformation" "Influence operations" "Cognitive security" "Media theory" "Tertiary orality" "The Gutenberg Parenthesis") "affiliation" (dict "@type" "Organization" "name" site.Params.affiliation "url" site.Params.affiliationURL) "alumniOf" (dict "@type" "CollegeOrUniversity" "name" "University of St Andrews" "url" "https://www.st-andrews.ac.uk/") -}} {{- if $sameAs }}{{ $schema = merge $schema (dict "sameAs" $sameAs) }}{{ end -}}
